# 0.11.0

An app with its own contrast and motion settings can now pass them to the package with `data-contrast` and `data-motion` on `<html>`. Under the system's reduced-motion setting, what moves still stops at once, but color and opacity now fade as before.

## Added

- **`data-contrast` and `data-motion` on `<html>`.** A page cannot change what `prefers-contrast` or `prefers-reduced-motion` reports, so an app's own settings had no way to reach the package. When one of these attributes is set, it decides in place of the matching media query, and with neither set the query decides, as before. `data-contrast="more"` draws the increased-contrast borders and `"standard"` leaves them out. `data-motion="full"` moves as with no preference, `"reduce"` does what the system's reduced-motion setting does, and `"none"` ends every animation and transition at once, after any delay it has. Forced colors and reduced transparency still follow the system, and rules inside a shadow root cannot see the attributes, though the heavier border color `data-contrast="more"` sets on `<html>` is inherited into one. See [Accessibility → App settings](../accessibility.md#app-settings). ([#582](https://github.com/codenhub/codenhub/issues/582))

- **Tailwind's `contrast-more:`, `contrast-less:`, `motion-safe:`, and `motion-reduce:` follow the attributes.** The `/tw` entrypoints replace the four variants as they replace `dark:`, so your own utilities follow `data-contrast` and `data-motion` where the package's components do. With neither attribute set, they apply as Tailwind's own do. See [Tailwind → Contrast and motion variants](../integrating/tailwind.md#contrast-and-motion-variants).

## Changed

- **Breaking: reduced motion keeps color and opacity fades.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, components and the reset stop what moves (a transform, a slide, a change of size), and what changes color or opacity still fades. Before, every transition and animation ended at once for those readers. `data-motion="none"` does that now, but for every reader, whatever the system asks: to keep the old behavior, set it from a script only while `matchMedia("(prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)").matches` is true, and update it on the query's `change` event. Under `data-motion="none"`, as under reduced motion in 0.10.0, every element whose style changes runs a 0.01ms transition, so a size read right after the change returns the old value. And `transition-property: none` alone, as in Tailwind's `motion-reduce:transition-none`, no longer turns a transition off under reduced motion, since the reset replaces it and keeps your duration: set the duration to zero instead, as in `motion-reduce:duration-0`. See [Accessibility → Motion and transparency](../accessibility.md#motion-and-transparency).
- **Breaking: `not-motion-safe:`, `not-motion-reduce:`, and `not-contrast-more:` compile to nothing under `/tw`.** Tailwind cannot negate the variants the `/tw` entrypoints now replace, so these utilities are dropped without a warning, as `not-dark:` already was. Write `motion-reduce:` for `not-motion-safe:` and `motion-safe:` for `not-motion-reduce:`, and in place of `not-contrast-more:border-4`, write `border-4 contrast-more:border-2`. See [Tailwind → Contrast and motion variants](../integrating/tailwind.md#contrast-and-motion-variants).
- **Breaking: a transition the reset removes fires no `transitionend`.** Under reduced motion, a transition of a transform, a position, or a size no longer runs, so code that waits for its `transitionend`, such as a drawer that removes itself once it has slid away, waits forever. Check whether the element still has a transition to run, or remove it after a timeout too. Under `data-motion="none"`, every transition still runs for 0.01ms, and the event fires.
- **Under reduced motion, an indeterminate bar fills its track.** It used to rest at a third of the track, which looked like a bar a third done. It now stops sweeping and fills the whole track, dimmed and still, under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` and `data-motion="reduce"` or `"none"`. A plain `<progress>` with no `value` does the same. See [Feedback](../usage/feedback.md).
- **Breaking: `.pixel-solo` clips only an action.** Its stepped corner was a `clip-path` on every element, so a pane or toast carrying it cut off a menu opening from it, a badge pinned to its edge, and a focused child's ring. A `button`, link, `[role="button"]`, `summary`, or button-type `input` keeps the stepped corner; anything else is square with the ring, as a surface is under `.pixel`. So a `.card.pixel-solo` or `.alert.pixel-solo` stepped in 0.10.0 is now square. A stepped corner on anything but an action is not supported.
- **The reset keeps your durations, in the order you wrote them.** It replaces the properties a transition names with color, opacity, visibility, `display`, and `overlay`, so a popover or dialog that fades out still fades before it closes, and the browser reuses the durations in order. So `transition: transform 200ms, opacity 1s` fades the opacity over 200ms, and what used to change at once now fades too: a background color over 1s, and a text color over 200ms. Give a transition one duration when the length of its fade matters.
- **An essential animation kept with a layer before `base` also needs the app's attribute.** A rule written under `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` alone does not apply when the app sets `data-motion="reduce"` or `"none"`. Write it again under `:root:is([data-motion="reduce"], [data-motion="none"])`, as the example in [Accessibility](../accessibility.md#motion-and-transparency) shows.
- **`chs build` refuses an `--output` it did not write.** The output is replaced whole, so `--content index.html --output index.html`, or an `--output` naming a stylesheet written by hand, lost that file. The command now refuses an `--output` that a `--content` entry names, an existing file that does not start with the package's `/*! @codenhub/styles` notice and holds more than whitespace, a file it cannot read, and `-`, and exits with status 1 without writing. A stylesheet it wrote since 0.9.0, and a copy of `@codenhub/styles`, `/components`, or `/native`, is replaced as it was. See [Without a bundler](../integrating/without-a-bundler.md#when-the-build-fails).
- **Breaking: a `chs build` wildcard stays out of `node_modules`.** Run from a project's root, `--content "**/*.html"` read every dependency's demo pages, and `**/*` read the package's own list of every class, so the whole stylesheet shipped. A `*` or `**` no longer enters a `node_modules` folder, and a glob that matched only files there now fails, naming it. To read a dependency's templates, name the folder, as in `--content "node_modules/some-ui/**/*.html"`. See [Without a bundler](../integrating/without-a-bundler.md).
- **`chs build` writes through an `--output` that is a link.** It used to replace the link with a file and leave the stylesheet it led to as it was. It now replaces the file the link leads to, creating it when it does not exist yet, and no longer reads that file back as content when a `--content` glob matches a link to it.
- **Breaking: a long `.btn` label wraps.** A button kept its label on one line whatever its container, so on a 320px screen a long label scrolled the page sideways. A label stays on one line while it fits, then wraps, balanced, when its container is narrower. A button in a flex row still shrinks when the row runs out of room, so its label may wrap onto two lines instead of pushing its neighbors out; an icon button keeps its square. Add `white-space: nowrap` to keep a label on one line. A label wraps between words only, so a single word wider than its container still overflows. See [Buttons](../usage/buttons.md).
- **Breaking: `.tooltip-bubble` hides and positions itself only inside `.tooltip`.** On its own, the class is now only the look, so a bubble that a positioning library or a `popover` opens shows when it is open. A bubble outside `.tooltip` that relied on the class to stay hidden needs hiding of its own. See [Tooltips](../usage/tooltips.md).
- **The package adds no letter spacing in scripts that join their letters or hang them from a headstroke.** The body, label, and title tracking broke Arabic ligatures, and WebKit spaced every letter; in Devanagari, Bengali, and Gurmukhi it cut the line along the top of each word. In Arabic script, Syriac, N'Ko, Mongolian, Adlam, and in Hindi, Marathi, Nepali, Sanskrit, Maithili, Bhojpuri, Konkani, Dogri, Bodo, Newari, Bengali, Assamese, and Punjabi, and in the common script subtags such as `ms-Arab` and `mni-Beng`, set with `lang`, the text styles, a field's `.label` and `.hint`, and an aesthetic's label tracking leave the spacing at `normal`, and under `/native` such a passage no longer inherits the page's tracking. Your own `tracking-*` utility still wins.
- **Under reduced motion, a popover's `display` and `overlay` stay discrete in any order.** The reset replaces the properties a transition names with its own list, and the durations, delays, and easings you wrote pair with that list by position. A popover written as `opacity, transform, overlay, display` lost its `allow-discrete` and closed at once. On a popover, a dialog, and a `::backdrop`, the reset now always transitions `display` and `overlay` discretely; every other element keeps the `transition-behavior` you wrote, so an element set to `display: none` still hides at once. [Accessibility → Motion and transparency](../accessibility.md#motion-and-transparency) lists the reset's order and how to write a transition that keeps its fade.
- **`chs build` refuses `--output` given twice.** `-o a.css -o b.css` wrote only `b.css` and said nothing about `a.css`.
- **`chs build` refuses an `--output` that names a folder.** `-o newdir/`, `-o newdir/.`, or `-o newdir/sub/..`, where `newdir` did not exist, wrote a file named `newdir`.

## Fixed

- **A radio's dot grows in.** The dot is sized with `scale`, and its transition named `transform`, which does not cover `scale`, so a checked radio's dot appeared at full size at once and only faded. It now grows over the same duration as its fade, and under reduced motion it only fades, as [Accessibility → Motion and transparency](../accessibility.md#motion-and-transparency) says.
- **A hovered neutral edge on a solid surface keeps the surface's ink.** A `.btn.ghost.edged` in a `.card.solid.primary` drew its border in the parent's ink at rest, but on hover the border faded to the hover tint of the fill it sat on, under 2:1 against it. It now keeps the parent's ink on hover too.
- **An `<input>` with no `type` gets the forced-colors focus ring.** In forced colors, the reset drew its `Highlight` ring outside a focused text field, but an `<input>` with no `type` attribute, which is a text field all the same, fell back to the generic ring drawn over its edge. It now gets the same ring as `type="text"`.
- **A focused control on a solid surface shows its focus.** The focus ring is the primary color, so a ghost button, a soft button, or a field focused in a `.card.solid.primary` drew it over a fill of its own color and showed no ring at all, and on other intents it reached 1.9 to 3:1. A neutral control on a solid surface now draws a line in the surface's ink inside its ring, the way a filled control draws one in the page's color.
- **A neutral box on a solid surface keeps its text legible.** A neutral box held by a solid surface is tinted with the surface's ink, and the tint lowered the contrast of that ink: a soft button, a soft badge, or a solid field in a dark `.card.solid.success` read at 3.6 to 4.2:1. Held this way, a box now fills at most 6%, at rest and hovered, and its text reads at 4.5:1 wherever the surface's own text does. ([#600](https://github.com/codenhub/codenhub/issues/600))
- **An unchecked toggle on a solid surface shows its boundary.** A `.checkbox`, `.radio`, or `.switch` with no intent drew its line in the page's ink on a `.card.solid`, at 1.03 to 2.3:1 against it, and a switch's knob in the page's grey. Both are now the surface's ink, at 4.4:1 or more, and so is the line inside its focus ring.
- **Increased contrast reaches a field on a solid surface.** Under `prefers-contrast: more` or `data-contrast="more"`, an `.ipt`, `.textarea`, `.select`, or `.input-group` on a `.card.solid` drew the page's full control border, at 1.0 to 1.6:1 against most intents. It now draws the surface's ink, and without the preference its quiet resting border is a fraction of that ink.
- **A placeholder reads on its field.** A placeholder was always the page's secondary ink, which a fill lowers: on a dark `.ipt.solid` it read at 4.4:1, and in a neutral field on a `.card.solid` it read at 1 to 3:1. It now leans toward the field's own ink as the fill grows, and on a solid surface it prints in the surface's ink, as the field's text does. There it looks like a typed value, since a lighter step would fall below 4.5:1 on some intents, so keep the field's label visible rather than relying on the placeholder.
- **A `/tw` source with the checkbox marks carries their licence.** The compiled stylesheets named the Lucide and Feather licences of the checkbox marks, and the `/tw` sources that hold the same marks did not. They now carry the same `/*!` comment. A minifier can still drop it from a `/tw` build, so a build that ships the checkbox keeps the notices in [NOTICE](../../NOTICE) some other way.
- **A switch under `/native` shows its knob.** `/native` styles every `<input type="checkbox">` as a checkbox, and that reached an `<input type="checkbox" class="switch">` too: the knob took the checkbox's mark, so an unchecked switch showed no knob and a checked one drew a tick, and a switch set to `indeterminate` took the "on" track with its knob off. A switch now draws as it does under the main entrypoint.
- **A focused toggle draws one ring.** A `.checkbox`, `.radio`, or `.switch` draws its focus ring as a shadow, and the reset's outline was drawn over it a pixel apart, so the two blotched into one uneven band. The outline is gone; forced colors still draw their `Highlight` outline. Under `.pixel`, the inset focus layers covered all but a dot of a 16px box, so a focused unchecked checkbox read as a filled one. They now stop two pixels inside the edge.
- **A toggle in an `.input-group` is not taken for its field.** Four of the group's rules took any `<input>` for its field: a focused checkbox in a group drew its ring inside the group's, an `aria-invalid` one turned the group's line destructive around neutral text, and a disabled one stopped the group reacting to hover without dimming it. A checkbox, radio, switch, or button-type input in a group is now its other content, as a button is, and keeps its own ring, in forced colors too.
- **A focused `.input-group` keeps its focus line under the pointer.** A lone field hovered while focused keeps its focus line, and a group's hover tone replaced it, so pointing at the field you were typing in faded its line.
- **An invalid control stays red on a solid surface.** On a `.card.solid`, a field, checkbox, radio, or switch with `aria-invalid="true"` took the surface's text color, as a neutral control does, so its red read as the valid gray. It is now drawn as its `.destructive` version. The field inside an invalid `.input-group` prints the surface's text color, as a field in an `.input-group.destructive` does.
- **Helper text is legible on a solid surface.** A `.hint` with no intent, an `.input-group`'s prefix, suffix, and icon, a table `<caption>`, and `.quote-inline` kept the page's secondary gray, which read at 1.0 to 3.2:1 on a solid intent. They now take the surface's text color, as a placeholder does.
- **A focused link, `<summary>`, or `[tabindex]` region shows its ring on a solid surface.** The default ring is the primary color at 64%, which drew at 1.0 to 2.9:1 there. It now takes the surface's text color. A `--focus-ring` you set is left as it is.
- **Under `.pixel`, a small focused icon button keeps its icon visible.** The inset focus layers reached 9px into every box, so a focused `.p-xs` icon button showed only a quarter of its icon. The ring now thins with the button's size.
- **In forced colors, a focused `.input-group` shows a `Highlight` ring.** It is a 2px `Highlight` outline 2px outside the border, as on a lone field. Before, the group's ring lay on its own border in the forced text color, so a focused group looked like a resting one.
- **Glass's dividers and table rules meet 3:1 under increased contrast.** Under `prefers-contrast: more` or `data-contrast="more"`, glass's surface line took the control gray, but its dividers and table rules read an ink of their own and stayed a 20% hairline, under 2:1. They now take the same gray.
- **`chs build` on Windows retries a write another program briefly holds.** An antivirus scan or a dev server that had the stylesheet open made the command fail with `EPERM`; it now tries again for a moment first.
- **`chs build` refuses an `--output` that is not a file.** It renamed the stylesheet over whatever `--output` named, so a pipe was replaced by a file, and as root `--output /dev/null`, or a link to it, turned `/dev/null` into a stylesheet. A folder failed with a raw `EISDIR`, or `EPERM` on Windows. After following any link, a folder, a device, a pipe, or a socket is now refused, with status 1, and nothing is written.
- **A failed `chs build` names the `--output` you gave.** The stylesheet is written to a temporary file beside the output, so Node.js's error named that file, as in `open 'public/app.css.706.tmp'` from 0.10.0. The line is now `cannot write --output <file>: <reason> (<code>); <what to do>`.
- **`chs build` keeps the mode and owner of the stylesheet it replaces.** On Linux and macOS, a `0640` stylesheet of the web server's group came back `0644` and owned by whoever ran the build. It now keeps its permissions, owner, and group. When the runner may not set the owner, as only root may, the file keeps its group when the runner is in it, keeps its permissions, and the command prints a `chs: warning:` line and still succeeds. A runner that owns the file but is not in its group keeps the owner, and the warning names the group alone. When the previous owner or group read the file through bits the change took from them, as the web server's group reads a `0640` file, the warning says they may no longer be able to read it.
- **`chs build` reads classes from a UTF-16 file.** Windows PowerShell 5.1's `"card" > safelist.txt` writes UTF-16 with a byte order mark, which was read as UTF-8, so every class in it was lost. A file that starts with a UTF-16 mark is now read as UTF-16, and a UTF-8 mark is dropped.
- **`chs build` reads a large file in less memory.** It split each content file into an array of every word in it before looking for classes, which held several times the file's size beside the text. It now reads the words one at a time.
- **`chs build` no longer writes through a link planted beside the output.** The temporary file was `<output>.<pid>.tmp`, a name anyone could guess, and the write followed a link put there, so whoever could write to the output's folder could make a build overwrite any file the runner can write, and on Linux and macOS give it another owner and mode. The name is now random, and the file is created only when nothing, a link included, is at that name.
- **`chs build` reads a word with many colons without crashing.** A word was split at every colon into one array, so a 160 MB content file of colons aborted Node.js with no `chs:` line. The parts are now read one at a time.
- **A `--content` glob that matches only the `--output` fails.** After a build into `public/s.css`, `--content "public/*.css"` read nothing and wrote a stylesheet without component classes. It now fails and names the glob. See [Without a bundler](../integrating/without-a-bundler.md#when-the-build-fails).
- **`chs build` does not read a temporary file a stopped build left.** A build killed before it renamed its temporary file left `<output>.<random>.tmp` beside the output, and a glob over that folder, such as `public/*`, read it as content and kept the last build's classes. It is now left out, as the output is.
- **`chs build` says why a `--content` entry matched nothing.** A folder gets a glob for its files, as in `app/views/**/*`; a wildcard whose only matches were in `node_modules` says it skips that folder; and an entry with a `[` gets the `[[]` spelling when that spelling matches a file, which matches a bracket on every system, where `\[` does not on Windows.
- **When the owner changes, the ownership warning says the previous owner may lose read access.** A `0600` stylesheet of the web server's came back owned by the deploy user, which the server could no longer read, under a warning that said only that its mode was kept.
- **`--no-minify` keeps a custom property whose value is a space.** `--_d-holds-ink: ;` came out as `--_d-holds-ink:;`, an empty value.
- **`chs build` explains a flag value that starts with a dash.** `-o -a.css` now says to write `--output=-a.css`; a value left out before another option, as in `-c -o out.css`, is reported as missing rather than as `--content=-o`.
- **A dialog's `::backdrop` and view transitions stop under reduced motion.** The reset reached only elements and their `::before` and `::after`, so a backdrop's animation and a view transition's movement ran under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, `data-motion="reduce"`, and `data-motion="none"`. They now end at once, an animation the page sets on the transition's root or an image pair too, and one that repeats runs once, since an endless one never let the transition finish.
- **A filled success button stays legible under the pointer in dark mode.** Its hover color, `#008e66`, printed the label at 3.98:1. It is now `#006448`, at 6.87:1: a darker green, where the other intents lighten on hover in dark mode. Every success hover tone in dark mode moves with it, soft and ghost included, and so do the dark `--palette-success-*-hover` values in `/palette`.
- **Under `.glass`, an open tooltip paints over the next surface.** Each glass surface is a layer of its own, so a bubble that overhung the next card, panel, alert, or table was drawn beneath it. Under glass, surfaces are now `position: relative` at rest, and one holding a tooltip pinned open, or hovered or holding focus with one inside, steps up to `z-index: 1`: above the next surface, under a sticky header or an open menu. Other aesthetics are unaffected. A bubble fading out after the pointer leaves still fades beneath the next surface.
- **Under `.glass`, a pane inside a filled surface stays legible.** A ghost or soft card, a panel, or an alert inside a `.card.solid.primary` showed the fill through its translucent pane and printed its text at 3.0 to 3.99:1. On a filled surface the pane is now opaque. A ground you set yourself with `--ui-surface-ground` is replaced by the page's color there too, even an opaque one.
- **`.btn.lg` stands as tall as `.ipt.lg` under a 2px border.** Under `.neobrutalism`, `.chunky-tile`, or a `--border-width: 2px` preset, it measured 49.5px beside the field's 48px.
- **The Tailwind guide names the order the package's variants need.** Written after a pseudo-element variant, as in `before:motion-reduce:hidden` or `placeholder:dark:text-white`, `dark:`, `contrast-more:`, `contrast-less:`, `motion-safe:`, and `motion-reduce:` compile to a selector browsers drop, so the utility ignores the app's settings. Write them first, as in `motion-reduce:before:hidden`. See [Tailwind](../integrating/tailwind.md).
