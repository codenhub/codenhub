## Brand Guidelines

The guidelines let someone outside the team produce new material that looks and sounds like the brand. Write rules and give examples; a gallery of mockups without rules cannot be followed.

Structure them in this order, leaving out sections the brand does not use:

1. **Brand core:** the positioning, brand idea, personality scales, and values, on one or two pages.
2. **Voice:** the voice principles with their right and wrong rewrites, tone by situation, a word list of preferred and avoided terms, and the messaging descriptions.
3. **Logo:** each version and when to use it, clear space defined by a part of the mark, minimum size in pixels and millimeters, approved color versions and backgrounds, and three to six real misuses.
4. **Color:** each color with its role, its values, and its proportion of use, plus the approved text and background pairings with their contrast ratios.
5. **Typography:** each family and its role, the type scale with sizes, weights, and line heights for each level, the fallback fonts, and where to get or license them.
6. **Imagery:** photography or illustration style with examples that follow it and examples that do not, and the icon style.
7. **Graphic elements:** each device, how it is built, and how it may be used and scaled.
8. **Layout:** grids, margins, and composition principles for the main formats.
9. **Applications:** the rendered touchpoints, each annotated with the rules it applies.
10. **Assets:** the list of files provided and where to find them.

## Guideline Rules

- State each rule as an instruction with a measurable value where one exists: "Clear space equals the height of the symbol", not "Give the logo room to breathe".
- Show a correct example next to each rule that is easy to break.
- Show only misuses that people actually commit, such as stretching, recoloring, placing on busy photos, adding effects, and rebuilding the wordmark in another font.
- Deliver the guidelines in a format the client can open, search, and share, such as a PDF or a web page, along with the asset files: logo in SVG, PNG, and print formats, color values as a swatch file when the client uses design tools, and templates for frequent materials.

## Brand Presentation

The presentation sells the thinking before the visuals. A client who agrees with the strategy judges the identity against it; a client shown visuals first judges them on taste.

Structure it in this order:

1. **What we heard:** the brief in the client's own terms, so they recognize their business.
2. **What we found:** the competitor map and the gap the brand can own, shown visually.
3. **Strategy:** the positioning, the brand idea, and the personality, one idea per slide.
4. **Directions or identity:** at concept stage, each direction with its name, its idea in one sentence, and its mood; at final stage, the chosen identity revealed through the logo and its idea, then color, type, imagery, and voice.
5. **In use:** the identity on the client's real touchpoints, with their real content.
6. **Next steps:** the decision needed from the client and what follows it.

## Presentation Rules

- One idea per slide, stated in its title as a sentence.
- Show the logo large and alone before showing it in context, then show it at small size to prove it works.
- Use the client's real content in every application: their product names, prices, headlines, and photos. Placeholder content makes the identity look generic and hides problems.
- Present each decision with its reason in the client's terms, tied to the strategy, not to design vocabulary.
- At concept stage, give each direction equal care. A presentation with one strong direction and two weak ones reads as manipulation and leaves no real choice.
- End with the one decision you need, not a summary.
