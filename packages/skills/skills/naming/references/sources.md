# Naming Sources

Seeds for each lane, with the technique and cautions that apply to it. The seeds are starting points to find related material from, not a menu to pick from. Names marked _(crowded)_ are already widely used in software; avoid them unless their story fits unusually well. Verify every meaning before recommending a name.

## Myth And Religion

Look for the figure, creature, place, or object whose specific story matches the thing's function or essence. The most famous god in a pantheon is usually taken; the helper, the object, or the place next to it often isn't.

- **Greek and Roman.** Ariadne (the thread through the labyrinth), Daedalus (the master craftsman), Talos (the bronze automaton that guarded Crete), Kairos (the opportune moment), Metis (cunning wisdom), Iris (messenger, the rainbow), Hestia (the hearth), Mnemosyne (memory; hard to spell), Janus (doorways and transitions), Argus (the hundred-eyed watcher). Crowded: Atlas, Apollo, Hermes, Athena, Titan, Prometheus, Olympus, Chronos.
- **Norse.** Huginn and Muninn (Odin's ravens, thought and memory), Mimir (the well of wisdom), Bifrost (the bridge between worlds), Gungnir (the spear that never misses), Sleipnir (the eight-legged horse, speed), Skadi (winter and the hunt), Brokkr (the dwarf smith). Crowded: Thor, Odin, Valhalla, Asgard, Loki.
- **Egyptian.** Thoth (writing and knowledge), Seshat (record-keeping and measurement), Maat (truth and balance), Ptah (craftsmen and creation through speech), Anubis (guardian of thresholds). Crowded: Ra, Osiris.
- **Mesopotamian.** Enki (wisdom, water, and crafts), Nisaba (writing and grain), Gilgamesh (the search for permanence), Ziggurat (the stepped temple).
- **Hindu and Buddhist.** Concepts travel well and offend less than deities: Karma, Dharma, Prana (breath, life force), Sutra (a thread, a teaching), Mantra, Bodhi (awakening), Zen and Chan (meditation). Figures such as Garuda (the swift carrier bird) and Vishvakarma (divine architect) are actively worshipped; see the caution below.
- **Chinese.** Nüwa (who mended the broken sky), Fuxi (who devised the trigrams), Wukong (the Monkey King of 72 transformations), Qilin (an auspicious creature), Pangu (who separated sky from earth), Tao, Qi.
- **Japanese.** Raijin (thunder), Fujin (wind), Inari (prosperity, with fox messengers), Kitsune (the fox spirit), Tengu (the mountain spirit), Kami (spirits), and concepts such as Kaizen (continuous improvement), Ikigai (reason for being), Wabi-sabi.
- **Celtic.** Lugh (skilled in every art), Brigid (smithcraft, poetry, and healing), the Dagda's cauldron (inexhaustible abundance), Ogham (the early Irish alphabet).
- **Slavic.** Perun (thunder), Veles (the underworld, cattle, and trade), Svarog (the celestial smith).
- **Yoruba.** Ogun (iron, tools, and technology), Eshu (crossroads and messages), Oshun (rivers and sweetness).
- **Polynesian.** Maui (the trickster who fished up islands and slowed the sun), Tangaroa (the sea).
- **Mesoamerican and Andean.** Inti (the Inca sun), Pacha (world, time, and earth in Quechua), Quipu (Inca knotted-cord records; see History).
- **Jewish, Christian, and Islamic.** Golem (the clay helper animated by a word), Ezra (help), Babel (the confusion of languages), Manna (provision from heaven), Ark, Eden, Genesis, Seraph, Gabriel (the messenger), Raphael (the healer), Sinai.

**Caution.** Using a figure who is worshipped today, such as Hindu deities, Christian and Islamic holy figures, or living sacred names, for a commercial product can read as disrespectful to believers. Prefer concepts, creatures, places, and figures from traditions that aren't practiced anymore, and say so when a candidate carries this risk.

## History

- **People.** First names often work better than surnames: they feel personal and are less entangled with estates (Claude, Ada, Grace). Seeds: Ada Lovelace, Alan Turing, Grace Hopper, Hypatia, Euclid, Archimedes, Kepler, Noether, Ramanujan, Fibonacci, Al-Khwarizmi (whose name gave us "algorithm"), Ibn Sina, Zheng He, Hero (Heron) of Alexandria, Babbage, Leibniz.
- **Places and empires.** Alexandria (the great library), Samarkand (the Silk Road crossroads), Timbuktu (manuscripts and learning), Byzantium, Carthage, Petra, Kyoto, Sparta (austerity, minimalism), Troy.
- **Artifacts and inventions.** Rosetta (the stone that unlocked translation), Antikythera (the ancient analog computer), Quipu (Inca knotted-cord records), Codex, Abacus, Astrolabe, Sextant, Loom (the Jacquard loom led to punch cards), Papyrus, Compass.

**Caution.** Some names of famous people are licensed and actively enforced, such as Einstein through his estate. Tesla and Nobel are already major brands. Prefer a first name, a work, or a discovery over a famous surname.

## Science And Phenomena

- **Elements and materials.** Helium, Neon, Argon (Greek for "idle"), Cobalt, Lithium, Carbon, Graphite, Tungsten, Mercury.
- **Minerals and rocks.** Obsidian, Basalt, Quartz, Onyx, Flint (the spark), Amber (it preserves), Garnet, Jade.
- **Physics.** Quark, Photon, Tachyon, Entropy, Inertia, Momentum, Flux, Doppler, Resonance.
- **Astronomy.** Pulsar, Quasar, Perihelion, Vega, Polaris (the guide star), Sirius, Kuiper, Lagrange (stable points). Crowded: Nova, Orion, Nebula, Zenith, Eclipse.
- **Biology.** Mycelium (the hidden network), Synapse, Axon, Symbiosis, Cambrian (the explosion of new forms), Chlorophyll.
- **Mathematics.** Googol, Fractal, Tensor, Euler, Sigma, Möbius, Lemma. Crowded: Vertex, Vector, Prime.
- **Weather and Earth.** Monsoon, Zephyr (the west wind), Sirocco, Aurora, Tide, Delta.
- **Cognition.** Gestalt, Flow (crowded), Kaizen, the Zeigarnik effect (unfinished tasks stay in the mind).

Pick a phenomenon whose behavior mirrors the thing's mechanism: mycelium for a mesh network, flint for something that starts things, amber for something that preserves.

## The Field Itself

Name an object, tool, trade, place, or action that is directly part of the thing's world, preferably the physical-world counterpart of a digital function.

- **Objects and tools.** Cursor, Blender, Anvil, Lathe, Chisel, Lens, Ledger, Loom, Spindle, Kiln.
- **Trades and roles.** Docker, Scribe, Courier, Pilot, Steward, Cartographer, Ferryman, Wright.
- **Places.** Harbor, Depot, Den, Atelier, Foundry, Quay, Hangar.
- **Actions.** Snap, Spot, Stitch, Sift, Weave.

These win on clarity and lose on ownership, since common words are rarely free in registries or at domains. They're strongest when the word is unexpected in its field.

## Language Roots

- **Latin.** Manus (hand), Lux (light), Iter (journey), Ferrum (iron), Ars (craft), Fides (trust), Opus (work), Verba (words). Crowded: Nexus, Veritas.
- **Greek.** Logos (word, reason), Kairos (the right moment), Techne (craft), Ethos, Meta (beyond), Kinesis (motion), Stoa.
- **Sanskrit.** Prana (breath), Sutra (thread), Veda (knowledge), Yantra (instrument, machine).
- **Japanese.** Sora (sky), Kaze (wind), Mizu (water), Hikari (light), Kumo (cloud, or spider), Ichi (one), Sumi (ink).
- **Romance languages.** Vista (view), Brio (vigor), Vela (sail), Faro (lighthouse), Alba (dawn), Fuoco (fire).

A foreign word reads as a coined name to most of the audience while still carrying a meaning for anyone who looks it up. Confirm the meaning and register with a native-level source, and check that it isn't slang or vulgar in a neighboring language or dialect.

## Arts And Culture

- **Music.** Opus, Fugue, Tempo, Cadence, Coda, Forte, Allegro, Chord, Riff.
- **Literature.** Sonnet, Haiku, Ode, Saga, Fable, Verse, Canto, Folio.
- **Archetypes and roles.** Rockstar, Ronin (the masterless samurai), Nomad, Maverick, Bard, Alchemist.

Forms make good families because they have a natural order, such as length, tempo, or scale.

## Nature

- **Animals.** Pick the animal whose behavior matches: Heron (patience, then precision), Kestrel (hovering, then diving), Lynx (sharp sight), Orca (intelligence and coordination), Mantis (stillness), Corvid (problem-solving), Gecko (grip). Animals give you a logo for free.
- **Plants.** Fern, Juniper, Moss, Bamboo (fast growth), Sequoia (scale, endurance).
- **Landscapes.** Fjord, Atoll, Ridge, Tundra, Mesa.

## Coined Forms

Coined names are the easiest to own and the hardest to make meaningful. Build them from a meaningful root so they still have a story.

- **Blend.** Join two words at a shared sound: Netflix, Pinterest, Microsoft, Instagram.
- **Truncate.** Cut a meaningful word down: Insta, Tumblr from tumbler.
- **Respell.** Change a real word's spelling: Google from googol, Lyft. Respellings that drop vowels make people ask "how do you spell that?".
- **Open-syllable coinage.** Alternate consonants and vowels into a word with no prior meaning, as in Xano, Hulu, and Zillow. Check that the result doesn't mean something in another language.
- **Acronym that reads as a word.** Tor works because it's sayable on its own. An acronym you have to spell out letter by letter rarely makes a good brand.
